# Educational Game Design Research

Five reference products were reviewed for their interaction patterns. These are design inputs; Nateiva keeps its own characters, curriculum, art, and visual language.

| Reference | Observed design pattern | Nateiva application |
|---|---|---|
| [ST Math](https://www.stmath.com/) | Visual puzzles let learners test a spatial idea and see the result in the world. Objectives build through levels and puzzle sets. | Keep the task action visible; make the math block matrix a playable answer space rather than a text quiz. |
| [DragonBox](https://www.dragonboxapp.com/) | Number characters and objects support several visual representations, with activities that change the way children manipulate numbers. | Present number concepts with movable objects and a different play loop for discovery, arcade, and mastery. |
| [Khan Academy Kids](https://www.khanacademy.org/kids) | Friendly character guides connect a large collection of reading, math, story, and creative activities into a learner path. | Use Léo and Kiki as recurring guides, keep spoken prompts close to the activity, and make subject exploration feel connected. |
| [PBS KIDS Games](https://pbskids.org/games) | A broad library uses varied short games, story settings, and recognizable characters across subjects. | Give each subject an art cue and vary its interaction instead of presenting the full library as one repeated quiz template. |
| [Prodigy Math](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/prodigy-math) | Curriculum questions move the player through an adventure with quests, rewards, and a visible game world. | Tie learning progress to the Nateiva expedition and reward moments, while keeping prompts short and the activity itself central. |

## Design Decisions

- Subject illustrations and animated state changes are part of the learning affordance, not decoration around a worksheet.
- The web arcade keeps French and English voice support, grade selection, progression, and the existing curriculum content.
- We use the references for interaction principles only; no characters, screens, or brand assets are copied.
