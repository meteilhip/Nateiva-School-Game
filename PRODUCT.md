# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are children ages 2–12 in Maternelle/Nursery through CM2/Primary 6 across Africa. They use the games at home and in classrooms, with French, English, or bilingual instruction. The youngest learners need audio-first activities and should be able to play without reading instructions.

## Product Purpose

Nateiva School Game is a bilingual educational arcade and interactive simulation suite. It helps children practise literacy, mathematics, science, geography, and general knowledge through short, curriculum-aware activities. Success means children can understand the task, play it independently or with spoken help, and make progress across distinct challenges.

## Positioning

The product combines French and English learning with African places, stories, examples, and curriculum needs in one arcade. Learning is meant to happen through the game action itself, supported by visual and spoken feedback.

## Operating Context

Children select a class/grade and subject, then complete activities and exercises through touch, mouse, keyboard, speech, or drag-and-drop. The arcade includes a 3-activity progression with 5 exercises per activity and records score/progress locally. Activities are used on phones, tablets, and desktop browsers, with a single local development host at port 8081.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The arcade includes math, reading, pronunciation, stories, science simulations, African geography, general knowledge, logic, and creative play.
- The project design matrix names 60 game mechanics across age bands; each game should keep its learning objective while using its own recognizable play interaction.
- Keep French/English content, class selection, grade-appropriate difficulty, audio narration, progression, and existing game content working.
- Preschool activities are audio-first. Minimize reading and writing where a visual selection, tap, drag, or spoken response can do the job.
- Keep touch targets comfortable, support keyboard and reduced-motion preferences, and do not rely on color alone.
- The web arcade is served from four synchronized HTML files: `index.html`, `arcade.html`, `public/index.html`, and `public/arcade.html`.

## Brand Commitments

- Keep the Nateiva School Game name and Léo/Kiki characters and story references already present in the product.
- Preserve the established red accent from the project identity. The user also requests a luxurious, fun, child-friendly experience with illustrated scenes and minimal writing.
- Use African settings and examples respectfully and in ways that support learning.

## Evidence on Hand

- `SchoolGameSoul.md` defines the audience, bilingual instruction, subject requirements, progression, tactile simulations, and four-file sync requirement.
- `GAME_DESIGN_MATRIX.md` documents 60 game concepts, mechanics, and grade bands.
- Existing arcade data contains bilingual stories, phonics, math, science, geography, and quiz content.
- The user requests distinct activities with more imagery, cartoon animation, discovery/adventure, and minimal writing.

## Product Principles

1. Make the learning action visible before asking a child to read about it.
2. Give every activity a distinct interaction and clear visual feedback.
3. Keep French and English narration available throughout the learning path.
4. Celebrate trying and discovery without punishing mistakes.
5. Ground examples in African settings and the learner's class level.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support audio-first use for early learners, large touch-friendly interactions, keyboard input, reduced motion, strong text contrast, and clear success/error cues that do not depend on color alone.
